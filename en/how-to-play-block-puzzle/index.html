How to play block puzzle: rules, scoring and high-score tips

Learn the rules in two minutes, then beat your high score with planning.

Learning how to play block puzzle games takes a minute: you place the blocks you are given on a square board, clear every row and column you fill, and keep scoring until none of your pieces fit anymore. The rules are simple, but a high score depends on the order and the spot you choose for every block. This guide covers the rules shared by block placement games, how scoring and combos work, 10 tips and tricks for a better score and how to keep the board from locking up. How to play block puzzle: rules, scoring and high-score tips

Block puzzle rules

In a block placement game the board is a grid of squares, usually 8×8 or 10×10. Three blocks wait in a tray below it. You drag them onto empty squares, and they stay exactly where you drop them: nothing falls, slides or moves afterwards. All the depth of the game comes from that one rule, because a block you place stays put until you complete a line through it.

  • When a row or a column is completely filled, it clears and those squares become empty again.
  • If a move fills a row and a column at the same time, both clear together.
  • You get three new blocks only after placing all three; you cannot skip a block or swap it.
  • The game ends when none of the blocks left in your tray fit anywhere on the board.
  • In our games blocks cannot be rotated; every block goes in exactly as it is shown.

How block puzzle scoring and combos work

In almost every game of this kind, points come from two places: the squares you place and the lines you clear. Lines are worth far more than placements, so the real goal is not to place blocks but to finish lines. Clearing more than one line in a single move earns a bonus. A combo, or streak, rewards clearing lines on consecutive moves, and a move that clears nothing resets it.

Here are the exact numbers in our games:

  • BlockNest: 10 points per square and 100 per line. Clearing 2 lines in one move adds +100, 3 lines +250, 4 or more +500. Consecutive clears multiply line points up to ×5, and emptying the whole board adds +300.
  • Woodora Classic: 10 points per square and 100 per line. Each extra line in the same move adds +50, and on consecutive clears a streak bonus grows by 25 each time (+25 on the second clear, +50 on the third).
  • Block Route in BlockFour: 10 points per square and 100 per line, with a combo that multiplies line points up to ×5. Multiple lines add +50 per extra line plus a +100, +250 or +500 bonus for 2, 3 or 4 lines.

10 tips and tricks for a higher score

  • Look at all three blocks before you move: find the order in which every piece still fits.
  • Place the hardest shape (the big square, a long bar, a big L) early, while there is still room, and keep small pieces for last.
  • Divide the board into zones in your head: saving one corner for squares and one edge for long bars makes every hand quicker to place.
  • In your first few games, focus on a tidy board rather than points; the score follows once the habits are there.
  • When a game ends, look at the final board: which shape ended it, and why was there no room for it? Make space for that shape earlier next time.
  • Since blocks do not rotate, keep separate space for horizontal and vertical long bars.
  • Finish a few lines instead of leaving many lines half full.
  • Look for placements that complete a crossing row and column in one move; two lines at once usually score more and open the board faster.
  • To keep a combo going, prepare several lines that are one square short, then close them one move after another.
  • Do not rush: in untimed modes you can think about every move, and one careless drop can end the whole game.

How to avoid locking up the board

A game rarely ends because the board is full. It ends because the shapes in your tray have nowhere to go. You can lose with half the board empty if that space is broken into scattered little gaps. The fix is to keep your empty space in one clean, connected area.

  • After every move, ask yourself: if the big square came now, where would it go? Where would a horizontal or vertical five-bar fit?
  • Avoid placements that split the open area in two; two small gaps accept far fewer shapes than one large one.
  • Once the board is more than half full, stop chasing points and clear the lines that free up space.
  • Different orientations of the same shape are different blocks, so never leave a gap counting on rotating a piece.
  • Long one-square-wide gaps along the edges are dangerous; close them early with bars or small blocks.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can you rotate the blocks?

It depends on the game. In BlockNest, Woodora Classic and Block Route in BlockFour, blocks cannot be rotated; every shape goes in as shown. Falling Blocks in BlockFour is a different kind of game, and there you do rotate the pieces.

When does a game end?

When none of the blocks left in your tray fit anywhere on the board. In BlockFour's Daily Route mode, the game ends after 30 moves.

What is a combo and how do you lose it?

A combo is a streak of moves that each clear at least one line. A move that clears nothing resets it. In BlockNest and Block Route the combo multiplies line points up to ×5; in Woodora Classic each consecutive clear adds a streak bonus.

Is there a timer?

BlockNest and Woodora Classic have no timer. In BlockFour, Block Route's Classic mode is untimed, while 120 Seconds is played against the clock.

Do I need an internet connection?

No. All three games play offline and need no account. Ad-based options and Google Play Games leaderboards do need a connection.

Which board size is easier for beginners?

For most players, 10×10. The extra space gives you a chance to recover from a mistake. An 8×8 board fills faster and makes every move count more.
