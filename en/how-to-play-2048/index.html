How to play 2048: rules and a winning strategy

You can learn the rules in a minute; the corner strategy brings the 2048 tile much closer.

How to play 2048 in one sentence: you slide tiles on a 4×4 board in one of four directions, merge matching numbers and try to build a tile that reads 2048. Learning the rules takes a minute; what separates a good run from a short one is where you keep your big tiles and which moves you avoid. This guide explains the rules with concrete examples, builds the corner strategy step by step, and covers the most common mistakes and what happens after 2048. The last section explains how the game works inside BlockFour, where it is called Number Merge.

2048 rules: what happens in one move

When you swipe right, left, up or down, every tile on the board slides in that direction as far as it can. A tile that runs into an identical number merges with it into a single tile worth their sum: two 2s make a 4, two 4s make an 8, and two 1024s make 2048. The value of every new tile is added to your score, so merging two 8s earns you 16 points.

One rule trips up a lot of new players: a tile can only merge once per move. That is why a row of 2-2-2-2 slid left becomes 4-4, not 8, with two empty cells opening on the right. In the same way, 2-2-4-empty slid left becomes 4-4; the freshly made 4 does not merge with the 4 next to it in the same move. A row of 4-empty-4-8 slid left becomes 8-8, and those two 8s merge into 16 on your next swipe left.

After every valid move a new tile appears in one of the empty cells, usually a 2 and sometimes a 4. A swipe that moves nothing does not count as a move and does not add a tile. The game is over when the board is full and no two equal tiles sit next to each other, either side by side or one above the other.

The corner strategy, step by step

Most experienced players rely on the same basic method: lock the biggest tile into one corner and line the other tiles up toward it in a chain that grows from small to large. The steps below use the bottom-left corner; if you prefer another corner, flip the directions to match.

  • Pick a corner and keep your biggest tile there for the whole game. With the bottom-left corner, your main directions are down and left.
  • Make most of your moves with those two directions. Use right only when neither down nor left does anything useful.
  • Save the swipe up for emergencies. Swiping up pulls the big tile out of its corner and can let a small tile slip in underneath it.
  • Keep the bottom row full. While the row is full and has no matching neighbors to merge, even a swipe to the right cannot move the corner tile.
  • Order the bottom row so the numbers shrink as they move away from the corner, for example 256-128-64-32. Merges then roll toward the corner like a chain reaction.
  • When the bottom row is complete, reverse direction on the row above: the chain climbs at the right end and keeps shrinking toward the left. This snake pattern keeps your large tiles next to each other.
  • Merge small tiles near the top of the board and bring them down to the end of the chain once they grow.

How to win 2048: small details that decide a game

The corner strategy gives you a structure; thinking a few moves ahead is what wins. Before every swipe, check two things: does this move shift the big tile, and where could the new tile land? New tiles only appear in empty cells, so a gap in the middle of your chain invites a 2 that breaks the order.

Try to keep plenty of empty cells. Empty cells are options; when the board starts to fill, merge small tiles first to make room. If two tiles of the same value end up in different corners, spending a few calm moves to bring them together is better than wrecking your chain in a hurry.

Finally, slow down. 2048 has no timer, and thinking through each move almost always beats fast swiping.

Common mistakes

  • Swiping at random. It seems to work in the first minute, but the board falls apart somewhere around 256.
  • Pulling the biggest tile out of its corner for one merge. A single swipe up can break the whole chain.
  • Trapping a small tile behind the corner row. Rescuing a 2 or 4 stuck between big tiles often takes many moves.
  • Filling the board completely. With few empty cells left, you can no longer control where the new tile lands.
  • Spending your undos early. The hardest moments usually come late in the game.

After 2048: high scores and bigger tiles

Reaching 2048 does not have to end the game. In BlockFour, as in many versions, a congratulations window opens when you make 2048; you can stop and go back to the menu, or keep playing and chase 4096 and beyond.

There are two ways to measure a great game: your score and your highest tile. Your score is the sum of every merge you make, so a long game with a tidy board scores more than a quick game with a lucky 1024. If you want to beat your record, stick with the corner strategy and avoid moves that clog the board.

Is there a 2048 cheat?

The honest answer is no. There is no secret code, no unlimited tile and no trick that clears the board. Most of what gets called a cheat online is either the corner strategy itself or the undo feature.

Undo exists in some versions and is completely fair. In BlockFour, undo takes back only your last move and also rewinds the random tile generator to where it was before that move. So if you undo and swipe the same way again, the same new tile appears in the same cell; you cannot use undo to fish for a better tile. Planning is still the only way to win.

2048 in BlockFour: Number Merge

Number Merge, one of the four games in BlockFour, follows the classic 2048 rules described above. Here are the details specific to the app:

  • The board is 4×4. Swipe with your finger or use the four direction buttons below the board.
  • About nine new tiles out of ten are 2s, and the rest are 4s.
  • You get 3 free undos in every game. Each undo takes back only your last move and cannot be used once the "No moves left" window appears, so use it before the board locks up.
  • When you reach 2048 you can finish and return to the menu, or keep going for bigger numbers.
  • When the game ends, you see your score and the highest tile you reached; the Records screen keeps your best score and your highest tile.
  • On Google Play Games there are achievements for the 512, 1024 and 2048 tiles and a leaderboard for your best score.
  • Your game saves automatically, works offline and needs no account.
Questions

Frequently asked questions

How do you win 2048, in short?

Keep your biggest tile in one corner, make most of your moves in two directions, keep the corner row full and line the numbers up so they shrink away from the corner. Take your time; there is no timer.

Why does 2-2-2-2 slid left not make 8?

Because a tile can only merge once per move. The first two 2s become a 4, the last two become another 4, and the row turns into 4-4. On the next swipe left, those two 4s merge into 8.

Does the game end when I reach 2048?

No. In BlockFour a congratulations window opens when you reach 2048; you can return to the menu or keep playing for 4096 and bigger tiles.

Is there a cheat for 2048?

No, there is no secret code. Undo is a fair helper: in BlockFour you can take back your last move 3 times per game, but repeating the same move brings the same new tile to the same cell.

Is the new tile always a 2?

Mostly. In BlockFour about nine new tiles out of ten are 2s and the rest are 4s, and a new tile always lands in an empty cell.

Can I play 2048 offline in BlockFour?

Yes. All four games in BlockFour, including Number Merge, work without an internet connection. The app is free and contains ads.
