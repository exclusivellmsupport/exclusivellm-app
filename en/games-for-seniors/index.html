What to look for in a game for an older player

  • No time pressure: games where you can think about each move as long as you like are far more relaxing than countdowns or blocks that speed up.
  • Readable pieces and text: big squares, clear color contrast and, ideally, a text size setting.
  • Works offline: the game should open even if the home Wi-Fi drops or mobile data runs out.
  • No account: an email, password or sign-up step is one of the biggest obstacles to playing without help.
  • Simple controls: one finger dragging or tapping should be enough; games that need several buttons at once can be frustrating.
  • Picks up where you left off: a phone call or a closed app should never wipe out a game.
  • Sound and vibration you can turn off: not everyone likes game sounds, especially around other people.

Free brain games for seniors: our games and why they fit

The four games below are ours, made by Exclusive LLM, and all are free on Google Play. They all contain ads; ads you watch to undo, continue or get an extra hint are optional. None of them needs an account, all of them play offline, and they keep progress on the phone.

Woodora Classic: a classic block puzzle on an 8×8 board, with no timer. If dragging is awkward, you can select a block and then tap the square where it should go, which is a real help for anyone who struggles to keep a finger on the screen while moving it. A preview shows how many lines a block will clear before you drop it. Sound and vibration can be switched off.

BlockNest: a calm wood block puzzle on a roomy 10×10 board, with no timer. In the settings, sound effects, vibration and reduced motion each have their own switch, and there are four wood themes: the first is open from the start, the others unlock as the best score grows, and you can pick whichever unlocked theme is easiest on the eyes. The game saves automatically and continues where it stopped, even after the app closes.

Exclusive Quiz: general knowledge questions in 10 categories. To be upfront: each question has a 20-second timer, so this is not an untimed game. On the other hand, text size can be set to Normal, Large or Extra large, the game turns dark when the phone is in dark mode, and sound turns off with one tap. The question timer stops when you leave the app. Questions are in English and Turkish.

BlockFour: four puzzles in one app. The most comfortable starting points for seniors are Block Route Classic, Number Merge (2048) and Color Sort with 50 levels, all without a time limit. The 120 Seconds mode and Falling Blocks, which speeds up, do add time pressure, so they are best saved for later. It has Pastel, Night and Wood themes plus sound, vibration and reduced motion settings.

Setting up a game on a parent's phone, step by step

Choosing the game is half the job. If you are setting one up on a parent's phone, a few steps together on the first day will save you many help calls in the weeks after.

  • Install the game from Google Play together; type the full game name when searching so it does not get mixed up with similarly named apps.
  • Open it for the first time while connected to the internet. In some countries an ad consent window may appear; make that choice together.
  • Play one full game together: how to drag a block, how a row clears, when the game ends and how to start a new one.
  • Switch the phone to airplane mode once and reopen the game, so you both see that it works offline, then switch airplane mode back off.
  • Set sound and vibration to taste; turning sound off is a good idea if they will play in shared spaces.
  • If text and buttons look small, increase font size and display size in the phone's display or accessibility settings; Exclusive Quiz also has its own text size option.
  • Move the game icon to the first page of the home screen, where it is easy to find.
  • Watch what happens when an ad appears, once, together: show how to close it and get back to the game.
  • Check notifications: going through the phone's notification settings together cuts down on confusing alerts from apps.

Small settings that make play more comfortable

  • Match screen brightness to the room; on a very dark screen, wood tones can blend together.
  • The reduced motion option in BlockNest and BlockFour tones down moving effects on screen; turn it on if animations are distracting.
  • A thick case that covers the screen edge can make the board's edge squares hard to tap; a slim case helps.
  • In Woodora Classic, if a finger hides the block while dragging, try select and tap instead.
  • In Exclusive Quiz, start with one category; familiar topics make the first rounds more fun.
Questions

Frequently asked questions

Are these games free?

Yes, all four are free to download on Google Play. They contain ads; watching an ad to undo, continue or get an extra hint is optional.

Do they need an account or email?

No. You open the app and start playing. Google Play Games features such as leaderboards use the Google account already on the phone; there is no separate sign-up.

Which games have no time limit?

BlockNest and Woodora Classic have no timer. In BlockFour, Block Route Classic, Number Merge and Color Sort are untimed, while 120 Seconds and Falling Blocks need speed. Exclusive Quiz gives 20 seconds per question.

Can the text be made bigger?

In Exclusive Quiz you can choose Normal, Large or Extra large text inside the game. The block games use little text and big squares; if needed, the phone's own display size setting makes everything on screen larger.

Do they work without internet?

Yes. All four play offline once installed and keep progress on the phone. Without internet, ad-based options such as an undo or an extra hint may not be available.
