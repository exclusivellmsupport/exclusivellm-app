Trivia questions

How these trivia questions with answers were chosen

The Exclusive Quiz question bank has more than 600 questions in every category, and each one is marked easy, medium or hard. For this page we picked only questions that do not go out of date: records, populations and anything described as "current" were left out on purpose.

The difficulty labels mirror the way a round works in the app. Easy questions are things most people have heard somewhere, medium ones ask for a bit of reading, and hard ones are aimed at fans of the category. If you want to keep score, note how many of the three you get in each category; missing the hard ones is perfectly normal.

Geography questions

Flags, capitals, mountains and lakes: geography questions often reward anyone who has looked at a map with care.

1. Easy: Which country's flag is white with a single red circle in the middle? A) South Korea; B) Japan; C) China; D) Bangladesh.

2. Medium: The Inca city of Machu Picchu is in which country? A) Chile; B) Bolivia; C) Peru; D) Ecuador.

3. Hard: Which African lake is the longest freshwater lake in the world? A) Lake Chad; B) Lake Malawi; C) Lake Victoria; D) Lake Tanganyika.

  • 1. Answer: B) Japan. The red disc stands for the sun; Japan is called the "Land of the Rising Sun".
  • 2. Answer: C) Peru. It was built in the 15th century high in the Andes, above the Urubamba valley.
  • 3. Answer: D) Lake Tanganyika. Lake Tanganyika is about 670 km long and is also the world's second-deepest lake.

Science questions

The Science category mixes things you see every day with discoveries that made history.

1. Easy: Which gas makes the bubbles in fizzy drinks? A) Oxygen; B) Helium; C) Carbon dioxide; D) Hydrogen.

2. Medium: Which process produces the energy of the Sun? A) Nuclear fission; B) Chemical batteries; C) Nuclear fusion; D) Burning coal.

3. Hard: Which two elements did Marie and Pierre Curie discover in 1898? A) Polonium and radium; B) Uranium and thorium; C) Helium and neon; D) Radon and francium.

  • 1. Answer: C) Carbon dioxide. The gas is dissolved under pressure; opening the bottle lets it escape as bubbles.
  • 2. Answer: C) Nuclear fusion. In the Sun's core, hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium at about 15 million °C.
  • 3. Answer: A) Polonium and radium. Polonium was named after Marie's homeland, Poland.

History questions

In history questions, knowing where and why something happened helps more than memorising dates.

1. Easy: In which year did World War II begin? A) 1914; B) 1937; C) 1939; D) 1941.

2. Medium: Which famous embroidered cloth tells the story of the Norman conquest of England? A) The Shroud of Turin; B) The Book of Kells; C) The Domesday Book; D) The Bayeux Tapestry.

3. Hard: In which German town did Martin Luther publish his 95 theses? A) Berlin; B) Wittenberg; C) Worms; D) Leipzig.

  • 1. Answer: C) 1939. World War II began when Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939.
  • 2. Answer: D) The Bayeux Tapestry. It is about 70 m long and shows Halley's Comet as an omen before the battle.
  • 3. Answer: B) Wittenberg. Tradition says he nailed them to the door of the Castle Church on 31 October 1517.

Nature questions

Nature questions are about animals, plants and the clever ways they adapt to where they live.

1. Easy: Which animal is known as the "ship of the desert"? A) Horse; B) Camel; C) Donkey; D) Elephant.

2. Medium: The Kangal, a large livestock guardian dog, comes from which country? A) Spain; B) Russia; C) Turkey; D) Mongolia.

3. Hard: Which Galápagos reptile is the only lizard that swims and feeds in the sea? A) Chameleon; B) Gila monster; C) Komodo dragon; D) Marine iguana.

  • 1. Answer: B) Camel. Camels can cross deserts for days without drinking, carrying people and goods.
  • 2. Answer: C) Turkey. Kangals guard sheep from wolves in central Anatolia and are named after a town in Sivas.
  • 3. Answer: D) Marine iguana. Marine iguanas dive to graze on seaweed and sneeze out the extra salt.

Sports questions

Sports questions mix rules, Olympic history and legendary players.

1. Easy: Which sport is played with small paddles and a light ball on a table divided by a net? A) Squash; B) Badminton; C) Table tennis; D) Padel.

2. Medium: In which year did the US men's basketball "Dream Team", with Michael Jordan and Magic Johnson, win Olympic gold? A) 1996; B) 1992; C) 1984; D) 2000.

3. Hard: FIFA's award for the most beautiful goal of the year is named after which player? A) Pelé; B) Ferenc Puskás; C) Alfredo Di Stéfano; D) Bobby Charlton.

  • 1. Answer: C) Table tennis. Table tennis, also called ping-pong, became an Olympic sport in 1988.
  • 2. Answer: B) 1992. Barcelona 1992 was the first Olympics where NBA professionals could play.
  • 3. Answer: B) Ferenc Puskás. The Hungarian striker scored 84 goals in 85 matches for his country.

General knowledge questions

The General knowledge category in the app brings together art, traditions and curious details from history.

1. Easy: Which Norwegian painter created "The Scream"? A) Gustav Klimt; B) Paul Cézanne; C) Edvard Munch; D) Claude Monet.

2. Medium: "Cinco de Mayo" (the Fifth of May) celebrates a victory by which country's army? A) Spain; B) Mexico; C) Chile; D) Argentina.

3. Hard: Who set up the first Ottoman printing press for Turkish books in 1727? A) İbrahim Müteferrika; B) Katip Çelebi; C) Evliya Çelebi; D) Piri Reis.

  • 1. Answer: C) Edvard Munch. Munch made several versions of The Scream between 1893 and 1910.
  • 2. Answer: B) Mexico. It marks the Battle of Puebla in 1862, when Mexico defeated a French army.
  • 3. Answer: A) İbrahim Müteferrika. İbrahim Müteferrika printed his first Turkish book, a dictionary, in 1729.

Film questions

The Film category covers classic directors, famous scenes and awards history.

1. Easy: Which director was known as "the Master of Suspense"? A) Stanley Kubrick; B) Alfred Hitchcock; C) Orson Welles; D) Steven Spielberg.

2. Medium: In "Back to the Future", which car is turned into a time machine? A) DeLorean; B) Chevrolet Corvette; C) Ford Mustang; D) Volkswagen Beetle.

3. Hard: Which Steven Spielberg film won the Oscar for Best Picture? A) Jaws; B) E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial; C) Schindler's List; D) Saving Private Ryan.

  • 1. Answer: B) Alfred Hitchcock. He made a brief cameo appearance in most of his own films.
  • 2. Answer: A) DeLorean. Doc Brown builds the time machine from a stainless-steel DeLorean DMC-12.
  • 3. Answer: C) Schindler's List. Schindler's List won seven Oscars in 1994; Saving Private Ryan lost Best Picture to Shakespeare in Love.

Music questions

Music questions range from world traditions to music theory and classical composers.

1. Easy: Mariachi bands, with trumpets, violins and guitars, are a tradition of which country? A) Mexico; B) Argentina; C) Spain; D) Cuba.

2. Medium: How many semitones are there in an octave? A) 7; B) 8; C) 12; D) 10.

3. Hard: What is the name of Beethoven's only opera? A) Fidelio; B) Der Freischütz; C) Don Giovanni; D) Tannhäuser.

  • 1. Answer: A) Mexico. Mariachi musicians often wear silver-studded charro suits and wide sombreros.
  • 2. Answer: C) 12. The twelve semitones correspond to the white and black keys from one C to the next.
  • 3. Answer: A) Fidelio. Beethoven revised it several times; the final version premiered in 1814.

Literature questions

The Literature category goes beyond matching authors and titles and asks about what happens inside the books.

1. Easy: Who wrote "The Old Man and the Sea"? A) William Faulkner; B) Ernest Hemingway; C) Jack London; D) John Steinbeck.

2. Medium: In Collodi's "Pinocchio", which creature tries to act as Pinocchio's conscience? A) A fox; B) A talking cricket; C) A blue fairy cat; D) A wise owl.

3. Hard: Which 14th-century collection has 100 tales told by young people escaping the plague in Florence? A) The Canterbury Tales; B) The Decameron; C) The Thousand and One Nights; D) The Heptameron.

  • 1. Answer: B) Ernest Hemingway. The short novel about the fisherman Santiago helped Hemingway win the Nobel Prize.
  • 2. Answer: B) A talking cricket. Disney named the character Jiminy Cricket in its 1940 film.
  • 3. Answer: B) The Decameron. Giovanni Boccaccio wrote it after the Black Death of 1348.

Technology questions

Technology questions are not only about computers; trains, early inventions and the origin of words belong here too.

1. Easy: Which country's high-speed trains are called Shinkansen, or "bullet trains"? A) China; B) France; C) Japan; D) Germany.

2. Medium: Leonardo da Vinci's "aerial screw" sketch was an early idea for which machine? A) Steam engine; B) Submarine; C) Helicopter; D) Bicycle.

3. Hard: The word "robot" was first used in a 1920 play by which Czech writer? A) Franz Kafka; B) Jaroslav Hašek; C) Karel Čapek; D) Milan Kundera.

  • 1. Answer: C) Japan. The first Shinkansen line, between Tokyo and Osaka, opened in 1964 for the Tokyo Olympics.
  • 2. Answer: C) Helicopter. Drawn in the 1480s, the aerial screw was meant to rise by spinning a linen-covered spiral.
  • 3. Answer: C) Karel Čapek. The play "R.U.R." used "robot" from the Czech "robota", meaning forced labour; his brother Josef suggested the word.

Easy vs hard multiple choice questions

Easy questions usually ask for one fact: a flag, a year, a name. In hard questions the options sit close together; Victoria, Malawi and Tanganyika are all lakes in the same region, and Kafka and Čapek both lived in Prague. That is why ruling out the options you are sure about is as useful as knowing the answer outright.

When you miss a question, reading the explanation pays off the next time something similar comes up. Anyone who learns about the Battle of Puebla once will not miss the Cinco de Mayo question again.
